Client Proposals & Pitching

Prompt 01 — Cold Proposal
Write a short, confident freelance proposal for a [web design / copywriting / SEO / video editing] project. My client is [describe client briefly, e.g. "a startup selling B2B SaaS"]. They need [describe the project in 1-2 sentences]. My relevant experience includes [2-3 bullet points of experience]. Keep it under 200 words, conversational tone, end with a clear call to action. No fluff.
Variables: [service type] [client description] [project description] [your experience]
Prompt 02 — Value-Based Proposal Angle
I'm a freelance [your role] pitching to [client type]. Instead of selling my time, help me reframe my proposal around outcomes. The client's core problem is [describe the problem]. The result I deliver is [describe the outcome]. Write a 3-paragraph proposal that opens with their pain, presents my solution as the bridge, and closes with a next step. Avoid jargon. Sound like a trusted advisor, not a vendor.
Variables: [your role] [client type] [core problem] [outcome delivered]
Prompt 03 — Follow-Up After Proposal Sent
Write a short follow-up email for a freelance proposal I sent [X days] ago. I haven't heard back. My service is [type of service]. The client's name is [name]. Keep it under 80 words, friendly but confident, not desperate. Add one soft new value point to remind them why it's worth replying. End with an easy yes/no question.
Variables: [days ago] [service type] [client name]
Prompt 04 — Competitive Differentiator Statement
Help me write a 2-sentence "why hire me over an agency or cheaper freelancer" statement for my [type of service] business. My unique strengths are [list 2-3 things]. My target client is [describe]. Make it punchy, honest, and something I could put in a proposal or LinkedIn bio.
Variables: [service type] [your strengths] [target client]
Prompt 05 — Upwork / Platform Profile Pitch
Write an Upwork overview for a freelance [role]. I specialize in [specific niche]. My top 3 results for past clients are: [result 1], [result 2], [result 3]. The tone should feel like a confident professional talking to a stressed business owner who needs fast, reliable help. Max 150 words. Start with the client's pain, not my credentials.
Variables: [role] [niche] [3 results]
Prompt 06 — Project Upsell Pitch
I'm working with a current client on [current project]. I see an opportunity to offer them [additional service] that would [explain the benefit]. Write a short upsell message — under 100 words — that introduces the idea naturally, explains the value, and invites them to consider it without any pressure. Sound like a proactive partner, not a salesperson.
Variables: [current project] [additional service] [benefit]
Prompt 07 — Price Increase Announcement
Help me write a message to existing clients announcing a price increase. My current rate is [old rate] and my new rate is [new rate], effective [date]. I want to keep the tone warm but firm — not apologetic. Acknowledge the relationship, explain briefly (1 sentence), and give them enough notice. Under 120 words.
Variables: [old rate] [new rate] [date]

Project Scoping & Discovery

Prompt 08 — Discovery Call Questions
Generate 10 high-quality discovery call questions for a freelance [your service] project. The client is in the [industry] space. I need to understand their goals, timeline, budget signals, decision-making process, and pain points. Questions should feel conversational, not like a form. Prioritize questions that reveal if this is a good fit.
Variables: [your service] [industry]
Prompt 09 — Project Brief Summary
I just had a call with a client. Here are my raw notes: [paste notes]. Summarize this into a structured project brief with: Project Goal, Target Audience, Key Deliverables, Timeline, Success Metrics, and Open Questions. Use bullet points under each heading. Highlight any red flags or unclear requirements in bold.
Variables: [raw call notes]
Prompt 10 — Scope of Work Document
Write a professional Scope of Work document for a freelance [service] project. The project includes: [list deliverables]. Timeline: [X weeks]. What's NOT included: [list exclusions]. Format it like a real contract section — clear, numbered, no ambiguity. Include an "Out of Scope" section and a revision policy (max [X] rounds of revisions).
Variables: [service] [deliverables] [timeline] [exclusions] [revisions]
Prompt 11 — Red Flag Detector
Read this client message and tell me: (1) are there any red flags I should be concerned about as a freelancer, (2) what questions should I ask before accepting this project, and (3) is the budget/scope realistic for [type of service]? Client message: "[paste message]"
Variables: [service type] [client message]
Prompt 12 — Project Timeline Builder
Create a realistic project timeline for a [type of project] with these deliverables: [list deliverables]. The deadline is [X weeks] from now. I work [X hours/week] on this project. Include buffer time, client feedback rounds, and a launch/delivery phase. Format as a week-by-week table.
Variables: [project type] [deliverables] [weeks] [hours/week]

Email & Client Communication

Prompt 13 — Late Payment Request
Write a firm but professional email requesting payment for invoice #[number], which was due [X days] ago. Amount: [amount]. The client is [describe relationship — new/ongoing/friendly]. This is my [first/second/third] reminder. Escalate the tone appropriately. Include the invoice details and a clear deadline for payment before I consider further action.
Variables: [invoice number] [days overdue] [amount] [relationship] [reminder number]
Prompt 14 — Scope Creep Response
My client is asking for [describe the extra request] which is outside our agreed scope. Write a polite but firm email that: (1) acknowledges their request positively, (2) clarifies that this falls outside the original scope, (3) offers to complete it as a separate paid add-on, and (4) keeps the relationship warm. Under 150 words. Don't apologize for enforcing the agreement.
Variables: [extra request description]
Prompt 15 — Project Status Update Email
Write a brief project status update email to a client. Project: [project name]. Completed this week: [list]. In progress: [list]. Blockers or items I need from client: [list]. Next milestone: [milestone and date]. Keep it scannable — use bullet points under each section. Professional but not robotic. Under 200 words.
Variables: [project name] [completed] [in progress] [blockers] [next milestone]
Prompt 16 — Difficult Client De-escalation
A client sent me this frustrated message: "[paste message]". They're upset about [describe issue]. Write a calm, professional response that: (1) validates their frustration without admitting fault, (2) explains what happened or what I'm doing about it, (3) gives them a concrete next step or resolution. Don't be defensive. Keep it under 150 words.
Variables: [client message] [issue description]
Prompt 17 — Client Offboarding Message
Write a professional end-of-project email to a client wrapping up the [project name] project. Include: a summary of what was delivered, what they have access to now, any handover notes, a testimonial request (soft, not pushy), and an invitation to work together again. Warm, professional tone. Under 200 words.
Variables: [project name]
Prompt 18 — Testimonial Request
Write a short, easy testimonial request message to a happy client who just finished a [type of project] with me. Make it feel like a favor, not a task. Give them an optional short template they can fill in if they're time-poor. Ask for permission to use it on my website and LinkedIn. Under 100 words.
Variables: [project type]
Prompt 19 — Contract Rejection Response
A potential client rejected my proposal, saying [give the reason they gave]. Write a gracious, brief reply that: thanks them, leaves the door open for the future, and asks one non-pushy clarifying question to understand if there's anything I could have done differently. Under 80 words. Don't beg or over-explain.
Variables: [reason for rejection]

Research & Competitive Analysis

Prompt 20 — Industry Deep Dive
I'm about to start a project for a client in the [industry] space. Give me a rapid briefing: (1) top 3 challenges this industry faces right now, (2) top 3 opportunities, (3) key terminology I should know, (4) 3 questions a savvy consultant would ask. Format as numbered lists. Keep each point to 1-2 sentences.
Variables: [industry]
Prompt 21 — Competitor Analysis Framework
My client is [company name] in the [industry] space. Their main competitors are [list 3-4 competitors]. Create a competitive analysis table comparing them on: pricing, target audience, key differentiator, content/marketing angle, and perceived weakness. Then give me 3 positioning angles my client could use to stand out.
Variables: [company] [industry] [competitors]
Prompt 22 — Target Audience Profile
Create a detailed customer persona for [product/service]. The business serves [describe the business]. Include: demographics, primary goals, daily frustrations, where they spend time online, what they Google before buying, and what objection they have before purchasing. Format as a persona card with named sections.
Variables: [product/service] [business description]
Prompt 23 — Market Positioning Statement
Write a positioning statement for [company/product] using this format: "For [target customer] who [need or opportunity], [product name] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [competitor/alternative], [product name] [key differentiator]." Then suggest 3 alternative taglines based on this positioning. Company details: [describe].
Variables: [company/product] [company details]
Prompt 24 — SEO Keyword Angle
My client runs a [type of business] targeting [audience]. Generate 20 SEO keyword ideas grouped by: (1) high-intent buyer keywords, (2) problem-aware keywords, (3) comparison keywords, and (4) long-tail informational keywords. For each keyword, add a brief note on why someone searching it would want [client's product/service].
Variables: [business type] [audience] [product/service]

Pricing, Rates & Invoicing

Prompt 25 — Rate Calculation Logic
Help me calculate my freelance hourly rate. My desired annual take-home income: [amount]. I plan to work [X] billable hours per week. I live in [country] and my estimated tax rate is [X%]. My monthly business expenses are [amount]. Add a 20% buffer for non-billable time. Show me the calculation step by step, then give my minimum viable hourly rate and recommended rate with 30% profit margin.
Variables: [income goal] [hours/week] [country] [tax rate] [expenses]
Prompt 26 — Project-Based Pricing Estimate
Estimate a fair project price for a [describe project] for a [small/medium/large] company in [industry]. My hourly rate is [rate]. I expect the project to take approximately [X hours]. Provide: a low estimate, a mid estimate, and a premium estimate with a justification for each. Also suggest what extras I could include in a "premium" tier to justify a higher price point.
Variables: [project] [company size] [industry] [rate] [hours]
Prompt 27 — Invoice Line Item Descriptions
Write professional invoice line item descriptions for the following work I completed: [list what you did in plain language]. Make each line item clear, specific, and value-focused — not generic ("consulting" or "design work"). The client should understand exactly what they paid for.
Variables: [list of work completed]
Prompt 28 — Retainer Proposal Structure
Help me structure a monthly retainer offer for [type of service] clients. I want to offer 3 tiers: Basic (~[price]), Standard (~[price]), and Premium (~[price]). For each tier, help me define: what's included, hours or deliverables per month, response time SLA, and one exclusive benefit for the higher tier. The value must feel obvious at each level.
Variables: [service type] [prices]
Prompt 29 — Handling "Your Rate Is Too High"
A client said my rate is too high. Their exact words: "[paste their message]". Write a confident, non-apologetic reply that: (1) acknowledges their budget concern, (2) reframes value vs. cost, (3) offers one alternative (smaller scope, phased payment, or trimmed deliverables) without undervaluing my work, and (4) holds my rate firm if they can't meet it. Under 150 words.
Variables: [client message]

Content & Portfolio Building

Prompt 30 — Case Study Structure
Write a freelance portfolio case study for a project where I [describe what you did] for [type of client]. The result was [describe outcome with numbers if possible]. Format it as: Client Context (1 sentence), Problem, My Approach (3 bullet points), Result, and a pull-quote I could attribute to the client. Keep it under 250 words. Make it feel real, not like an ad.
Variables: [what you did] [client type] [outcome]
Prompt 31 — LinkedIn About Section
Write a first-person LinkedIn "About" section for a freelance [role]. I specialize in [niche]. My ideal clients are [describe]. I'm known for [describe your working style/approach]. Top results I've achieved: [list 2-3]. My CTA: [e.g. DM me, visit my website]. Max 250 words. Open with a hook that isn't "I'm a freelancer." Sound like a real person, not a resume.
Variables: [role] [niche] [ideal clients] [working style] [results] [CTA]
Prompt 32 — Newsletter / Blog Content Idea Generator
Generate 15 content ideas for a freelance [role] who wants to build an audience of [target clients]. Each idea should be: (1) a specific, titled article or newsletter issue, (2) written from the angle of a practitioner sharing real experience (not generic tips), and (3) relevant to a problem [target clients] actually have. Format as a numbered list with the title and a one-line description.
Variables: [role] [target clients]
Prompt 33 — Services Page Copy
Write a Services page section for a freelance [role] website. The service is [service name]. Include: a headline (benefit-focused, not feature-focused), a 3-sentence description, 4-5 bullet points of what's included, who it's for (1 sentence), and a CTA button label. Avoid jargon. Speak to someone who knows they have a problem but isn't sure if this service solves it.
Variables: [role] [service name]
Prompt 34 — Testimonial Polish
A client gave me this raw testimonial: "[paste testimonial]". Help me: (1) identify the strongest quote I could pull out for social proof, (2) suggest a headline or label to put above it (e.g. "Increased revenue 40%"), and (3) note what's missing that would make it stronger (so I know what to ask for in the next testimonial).
Variables: [raw testimonial]
Prompt 35 — Cold Email Outreach Sequence
Write a 3-email cold outreach sequence targeting [type of company] for my [service] business. Email 1: Value opener, no ask (Day 1). Email 2: Soft ask with social proof (Day 4). Email 3: Final short "breaking up" email (Day 9). Each email under 100 words. Subject lines included. No templates-that-look-like-templates. Sound like a human who did their homework.
Variables: [company type] [service]

Marketing Your Services

The hooks should use different formats: question, bold claim, counterintuitive statement, "most people think X but actually Y", story opener, number-based, and failure/mistake angle. Each hook is 1-2 sentences max. I'll test which performs best.
Variables: [topic/niche]
Prompt 37 — Lead Magnet Idea Generator
I'm a freelance [role] targeting [client type]. Generate 8 lead magnet ideas I could create in a weekend to attract potential clients. For each, include: title, format (checklist/PDF/video/etc.), and why a [client type] would want it. Ideas should feel immediately useful, not generic. Avoid "10 tips" formats — aim for tools, calculators, templates, or systems.
Variables: [role] [client type]
Prompt 38 — Niche Down Statement
I'm a [general freelance role] and I want to niche down to attract better clients and charge more. My experience includes [list your background]. My best past projects were [describe 2-3]. Suggest 5 specific niches I could position in, with: (1) the niche, (2) the type of client, (3) a sample positioning line, and (4) one content angle I could use to attract them. Be specific, not generic.
Variables: [general role] [background] [best projects]
Prompt 39 — Referral Request Message
Write a message I can send to 3 types of people asking for referrals: (1) a past client I loved working with, (2) a fellow freelancer in a complementary field, (3) a friend who works in [target industry]. Each message under 80 words. Personal, not copy-paste sounding. Tell them exactly what kind of client I'm looking for so they can easily refer me.
Variables: [target industry]
Prompt 40 — Website Headline Options
Write 10 homepage headline options for a freelance [role] who specializes in [niche]. The ideal client is [describe]. The primary outcome I deliver is [outcome]. Mix these styles: outcome-focused, pain-focused, credibility-based, specific and bold, and curiosity-gap. Each headline max 10 words. Include a 1-sentence sub-headline for your top 3 picks.
Variables: [role] [niche] [ideal client] [outcome]
Prompt 41 — Podcast Pitch Email
Write a podcast pitch email targeting [type of podcast] shows. I want to be a guest to build credibility and attract clients. My topic: [topic]. My angle: [unique angle or experience]. Include: a subject line, a brief credential intro (2 sentences max), 3 specific episode topic ideas with one-line descriptions, and a no-pressure CTA. Max 200 words total. Sound like a professional peer, not a fan.
Variables: [podcast type] [topic] [angle]
Prompt 42 — Product/Service Launch Announcement
Write a launch announcement for my new [service/product]: [describe it]. Audience: [where I'm posting — email list, LinkedIn, Twitter, etc.]. Format: opening hook, what it is (1 sentence), who it's for and what problem it solves, what makes it different, price and availability, CTA. Under 200 words. Conversational, not hype-y. Make people feel like they'd be missing out if they ignore it.

Productivity & Workflow

Prompt 43 — Weekly Planning Structure
Design a weekly planning structure for a freelancer managing [X] active clients, working [X hours/day], and also trying to grow their business through marketing. The week has [X days]. Create a time-block template showing: client work hours, admin/invoicing, marketing/content creation, learning, and buffer time. Make it realistic — not a hustle-porn schedule.
Variables: [number of clients] [hours/day] [work days]
Prompt 44 — Client Workload Prioritization
I have [X] active clients with competing deadlines this week. Here's what each needs: [list each client and their deliverable with deadline]. Help me prioritize this work using an urgency/importance matrix. Suggest the order I should tackle each task and flag any risks (e.g. deadlines I might miss). Then draft a brief update email I can send to any client whose work is being delayed.
Variables: [client list with deadlines]
Prompt 45 — Meeting Notes → Action Items
Convert these raw meeting notes into a clean action item list: [paste meeting notes]. For each action item, include: what needs to be done, who is responsible (me or client), and the due date (or flag if no deadline was set). Then write a short follow-up email I can send to the client summarizing what was agreed. Under 200 words for the email.
Variables: [meeting notes]
Prompt 46 — Freelance SOP Template
Create a simple Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for [a recurring freelance process — e.g. onboarding a new client, delivering a final project, handling a revision request]. The SOP should include: trigger (when does this process start), steps in order, tools used, estimated time per step, and common mistakes to avoid. Format as a numbered checklist I can use every time.
Variables: [specific process to document]
Prompt 47 — Burnout Prevention Check-In
I'm a freelancer feeling [describe how you feel — overwhelmed, stuck, unmotivated, exhausted]. Here's what my week looks like: [describe your current workload]. Give me: (1) a diagnosis of what might be causing this, (2) 3 immediate changes I can make this week, and (3) a 30-day sustainable adjustment to my workload or habits. Be practical, not generic wellness advice.
Variables: [current state] [workload description]
Prompt 48 — Email Inbox Zero Template
I get [X] client emails per day. Help me write 5 reusable email reply templates I can keep in my drafts for: (1) acknowledging a request and setting a response timeline, (2) saying I'm unavailable until [date], (3) clarifying unclear client feedback, (4) politely pushing back on an unreasonable request, and (5) a quick "received, working on it" confirmation. Each under 60 words.
Variables: [email volume] [unavailability date]

Include: a warm confirmation, what happens next (3 steps), what I need from them in the first 48 hours, and a link to [your onboarding form or tool — or leave as placeholder]. Tone: professional but personable. Under 200 words.
Variables: [project type] [onboarding form link]

Create a 10-question client onboarding questionnaire for a freelance [service] project. Questions should uncover: their brand voice, target audience, examples they love/hate, technical constraints, key stakeholders, decision-making process, success definition, and any previous attempts to solve this problem. Make questions open-ended where insight is needed, multiple-choice where speed is preferred.
