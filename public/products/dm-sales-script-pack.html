The 4 Rules Before You Send Any DM

1

Warm the lead first. Interact with their content (like, reply to a story, comment genuinely) before DMing. Cold DMs from zero interaction = spam.

2

One ask per message. Never pitch the product AND ask a question AND invite them to check your page in the same message. One action, one message.

3

Replace the brackets. Every [bracket] needs a real, personalized answer. If you can't fill it in, you don't know enough about this person yet.

4

Send max 20 DMs/day. On Instagram, high-volume DMing triggers shadow restrictions. Quality over quantity — 5 great DMs beat 50 generic ones.

Script 01 of 10
The Value-First Opener
Use when: reaching out to someone who engaged with your content but hasn't bought yet
The Message
Hey [Name] 👋 Saw you [liked my post about / commented on / saved] the [topic] content — figured you'd appreciate this. I put together [name of free resource or tip] that expands on exactly that. Want me to send it over? No strings attached, just something I think you'd find useful.
Why it works: You're leading with value, not a pitch. The "no strings attached" line removes the transaction feeling. You're asking a yes/no question — low friction, high response rate.

✓ Do

  • Be specific about what they engaged with
  • Have the free resource actually ready
  • Only follow up once if no reply

✗ Don't

  • Immediately pitch your paid product after
  • Use this as a fake opener to sell
  • Send without personalizing [Name]
Script 02 of 10
The Problem-Solution Match
Use when: someone posted or commented about a specific struggle you can solve
The Message
Hey [Name], Saw your [comment/post] about struggling with [specific problem they mentioned] — that's exactly why I made [product name]. It's a [brief description: e.g. "PDF guide / template pack / prompt library"] that helps [target person] [solve the specific problem] without [the annoying part — e.g. spending hours on research / needing design skills]. Here's the link if you want to check it out: [link] No pressure — just thought it was relevant to what you shared.
Why it works: You're responding to something THEY said, not interrupting them with a pitch. The "no pressure" close keeps it low-stakes and human.

✓ Do

  • Quote their exact words from the post
  • Keep the product description one sentence
  • Send within 24h of their post

✗ Don't

  • Use a generic product description
  • Add "It's only $X!" — that sounds desperate
  • Pitch in a comment instead of DM
Script 03 of 10
The Social Proof Warm-Up
Use when: following up after they've seen your content multiple times but haven't bought
The Message
Hey [Name]! Wanted to share something quick — [Name of buyer or "someone from [their city/niche]"] just [result they got — e.g. "landed their first freelance client" / "set up their Gumroad store in one afternoon"] using [product name]. Thought of you because you [specific reason — e.g. "mentioned wanting to do the same thing" / "asked about this in your stories last week"]. If you've been on the fence, happy to answer any questions before you decide. What's holding you back?
Why it works: Social proof from a peer (not you) is 10x more convincing than you saying your product is great. Ending with a question opens a conversation instead of forcing a decision.

✓ Do

  • Use a real result from a real buyer
  • Make the peer relatable to this person
  • Keep the question open-ended

✗ Don't

  • Make up results
  • Use vague proof ("people love it")
  • Send without the "thought of you" connection
Script 04 of 10
The Collaboration Angle
Use when: reaching out to a peer creator in a complementary niche
The Message
Hey [Name] — love what you're building with [their content/product/page]. I make [what you make] for [your audience] — a lot of overlap with the people you reach. Would you be open to a quick trade? I'll share your [product/page] with my audience in exchange for you sharing mine. No money involved — just matching audiences with relevant content. Worth a quick chat?
Why it works: Framing it as a "trade" feels fair and non-extractive. You're building a partnership, not asking for a favor.

✓ Do

  • Verify you actually have audience overlap first
  • Mention something specific you like about their work
  • Start with a small collab before a big one

✗ Don't

  • Ask someone with 100k followers if you have 200
  • Propose this to direct competitors
  • Over-explain in the first message
Script 05 of 10
The Limited Availability Close
Use when: running a time-limited sale or bonus offer
The Message
Hey [Name] — just a heads up that [product name] is [on sale / includes a bonus / going back to full price] until [specific date/time]. I know you [engaged with it / asked about it / have been considering it] — didn't want you to miss the window. Here's the link: [link] After [date], [the price goes back to $X / the bonus disappears / the discount ends]. Just being transparent so you can decide with full info.
Why it works: Urgency works — but only when it's real. Fake urgency destroys trust permanently. "Just being transparent" frames you as on their side, not manipulating them.

✓ Do

  • Only use REAL deadlines and prices
  • Reference their previous interest specifically
  • Let the deadline be the closer — don't pile on more pressure

✗ Don't

  • Create fake urgency ("only 3 left" for a digital product)
  • Send this to everyone — only to warm leads
  • Follow up more than once on this
Script 06 of 10
The "I Made This For You" Personal Pitch
Use when: launching a new product to warm followers who match the exact target audience
The Message
Hey [Name] — I just dropped something I think is genuinely up your alley. I made [product name] specifically for [describe target person] who want to [achieve outcome] without [common obstacle]. Based on what you share, you're exactly who I had in mind when I built it. Here it is: [link] Would love to hear what you think — even if you don't buy it, curious if it resonates.
Why it works: "I had you in mind" is flattering and personalizing. Asking for their opinion (even without buying) removes pressure and often leads to either a sale or useful feedback.

✓ Do

  • Only send to people who truly match your ideal buyer
  • Actually want their feedback — be genuine
  • Send to max 10-15 people per launch

✗ Don't

  • Mass-send this — it must feel personal
  • Lead with the price in this message
  • Send to people you've never interacted with
Script 07 of 10
The Objection Handler
Use when: someone expressed interest but said "maybe later" or "I'll check it out"
The Message
Hey [Name] — quick follow-up on [product name]. I hear "I'll check it out" a lot, and I get it — there's always something else on the to-do list. But I'm curious: what would make you feel confident it's worth your time and money? Is it [price / relevance / whether it actually works / something else]? No pressure — I just want to understand if there's a real barrier or if it's just timing.
Why it works: Instead of pushing harder, you're inviting them to tell you their objection. Once you know the real reason, you can address it directly. Most people never get past "maybe later" because they never ask why.

✓ Do

  • List real, common objections in the brackets
  • Be genuinely curious — not interrogating
  • Wait at least 48h after their "maybe later"

✗ Don't

  • Send this more than once
  • Get defensive if they give a hard no
  • Immediately pitch a discount when they object to price
Script 08 of 10
The Testimonial Request (For Social Proof Building)
Use when: a buyer has had your product for 3-7 days
The Message
Hey [Name]! Checking in on [product name] — how are you finding it so far? If it's been helpful, I'd genuinely appreciate a quick 2-sentence review. Something honest about what you liked and what you got from it. I'd love to share it on my page (with your permission) and maybe tag you if you want the visibility. No pressure if the timing isn't right — just figured I'd ask while it's fresh!
Why it works: "2-sentence review" removes the effort barrier. Offering to tag them gives a benefit (exposure), making it a mutual exchange rather than a favor to you.

✓ Do

  • Check in on how they're finding it first (genuine interest)
  • Make the ask feel optional and light
  • Follow through — post it and tag them

✗ Don't

  • Ask for a review immediately after purchase
  • Send a formal "leave a review" link — too transactional
  • Ghost them after getting the testimonial
Script 09 of 10
The Soft Close After Story View
Use when: someone watches your "product teaser" story but doesn't click the link
The Message
Hey [Name] — noticed you watched the story about [product name] 👀 Totally fine if it's not for you — but if you had a question about it or weren't sure if it was relevant to your situation, I'm happy to answer before you decide. What's your current situation with [the problem your product solves]?
Why it works: Acknowledging the story view without being creepy ("noticed you watched") feels like genuine attention, not surveillance. Ending with a question about their situation makes it about them, not the sale.

✓ Do

  • Send within 6 hours of the story view
  • Keep it casual and light
  • Actually answer their question fully — don't deflect to a link

✗ Don't

  • Track every viewer and DM all of them (pick the most likely buyers)
  • Mention the price in this message
  • Send if you've already DM'd them about this product recently
Script 10 of 10
The Graceful No Follow-Up
Use when: someone said no or went quiet after a pitch
The Message
Hey [Name] — totally understand, no worries at all. If the timing ever changes or you come across a problem [product name] could help with, I'm around. In the meantime — anything I can help you with on the [topic] side that doesn't involve buying anything? Happy to point you in the right direction.
Why it works: The "anything I can help with" close keeps the relationship open without pressure. Many people who say no today become buyers 3 months later — but only if you didn't burn the relationship with a pushy follow-up. This script keeps the door open.

✓ Do

  • Actually be willing to help for free
  • Keep this message SHORT — no over-explaining
  • Move on mentally after sending this

✗ Don't

  • Follow up again after this
  • Make them feel guilty for saying no
  • Immediately pitch a different product
